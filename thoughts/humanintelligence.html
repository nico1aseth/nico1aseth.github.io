Human Intelligence

October 10, 2026

I was studying Agricultural Chemistry in college. My passion for chemistry stems from my first year in university, back when I was preparing for the transfer exam to the Department of Pharmacy and getting captivated by the scope and depth of basic chemistry like acid base titration, chem rate calc and electron theory. So later I decided to focus on Agri Chem. Experiment classes take up most of our loadings, plus there are pre-labs + in-class quiz + post-lab write-ups, so i ended up spending tons of extra time summarizing exp. results. (i.e why yield rate lower than expected) Today, LLMs can summarize the work much more efficiently and accurately than us, which leads me to think about what’s the value of the process in which humans acquire new skills and learn? The freshness in childhood when we get to learn new things and finally get to comprehend some knowledge points and finally grasping a difficult concept with that sudden click of clarity. But now LLM can easily answer most of all questions raised.

The process now: exposure to new knowledge -> try to understand -> confusion and contemplation -> see the light and internalize -> apply and scale up.

In this era, knowledge itself costs nothing. So do the answers. The point is: raise novel and important questions in different domains. I.e FAANG have always been refining their recommendation systems or CTR, but the newly releasing result GenRec can replace the previous rec sys in Netflix, which directly proves that LLM can do much more better in existing domains and proven solvable areas. However, there is still more to explore in this world. Only humans possess the capability to push the knowledge frontier to unprecedented scope. Tools that drastically change how our lives are like airplanes, w.w.w and even LLM all come from the creativity hand of humanity. To reflect on myself, I can’t imagine my prospective master studies without the use of LLM. From exploring and validating research questions selection -> raising hypothesis -> experimental design -> data analysis -> thesis writing LLM basically takes up every part of it and is especially good at performing hands-on jobs. BUT!!! It fails to tell the nuance where what truly matters? What are the crucial and worth answering problems? What problems are trivials? LLM training from Big Data and the essence is human experience and past data. Only humans can experience new things, tell meaningful stories that can genuinely resonate with people and show empathy to understand others. LLM can do next token prediction, but humans care deeply about the world with genuine intent, which is how we truly discover what benefits it.
